The synchronisation (`synth/sync.py`): the underlying's path inside the window is estimated from every option trade in it (the TBBO stream carries each option's own bid/ask at the trade), on 1-minute knots with a random-walk prior at the ATM vol, and its level is anchored to the settlement at 14:29 (parity at 10:30). `level offset` is the options-implied level at the anchor minus the futures — a second measurement of the Stage 6 disagreement that uses every quote; `anchor drift` is the path's last-minute move, i.e. forward used − settlement. The adjustment splits into a theta part (T_i → T_ref) and a delta part (F(t_i) → F_ref).

## 8. Verdicts

**χ²/strike first, because it is the headline metric.** On the same 30 dates, same snapshots, same sampler, Act III's fit to the market's own spreads at the 60-minute T-1d window went from a median of 8.1 (V1) to 6.4 with only the forward changed, to **1.6** with the quotes synchronised — against 2.0 at the near-synchronous 10-minute window in V1 and 1.1 on synthetic well-specified chains. The synchronised 60-minute chain now fits *better* than V1's 10-minute chain while keeping all 30 dates (V1's 10-minute window kept 9). At T-2d/60 it is 4.2 → 3.6 → **1.6** on 26 dates (V1 extracted 14); at T-4h/60, 5.7 → **1.9**.

### Fix 1 — forward from the futures, parity as a check: **partially worked, and not on its own**

- What it fixes: the location. V1's parity forward missed the settlement by 37.7¢ RMSE at T-1d/60 (within 10¢ on 32% of dates); the settlement is exact by construction. It also removes parity's data requirement, so dates with fewer than two two-sided pairs now extract: T-2d/60 goes from 14 to 26 dates, T-1d/10 from 9 to 21, T-2d/10 from 0 to 8.
- What it does not fix: the fit. With the raw window and the correct forward, χ²/strike at T-1d/60 only moves 8.1 → 6.4, and several diagnostics get *worse*: the Stage 14 unconstrained mean lands on the forward on 30% of dates instead of 36% (|z| median 5.2 vs 3.9), the bracket R̂ median rises from 1.038 to 1.065 and the bracket ESS falls from 135 to 88. That is the expected signature of quotes that were struck at a different underlying level than the forward they are now being fitted around: the *correct* forward with *asynchronous* quotes is a worse-conditioned problem than the wrong forward the raw chain happened to be centred on. The forward change only pays off together with fix 2.
- The check itself: the raw-parity-vs-futures flag fires on 46% of T-1d/60 dates and 60% at T-0/60 (|z| > 3 and > 10¢). After synchronisation the parity forward agrees with the settlement to 10.8¢ RMSE (within 10¢ on 68% of dates) and with the path forward the chain was actually fitted around to 7.6¢ RMSE (82% within 10¢). The residual disagreement is the honest size of "parity on this data": a few cents on most dates, 10–30¢ on the few where the last minutes were fast or the pairs were two.
- Judgement calls made: D is fixed from the rate (immaterial at T ≤ 2 days, and the slope had nothing left to estimate); the check reports rather than gates in this pass, because on these chains it fires on stale quotes that synchronisation then repairs — in production it should gate, on the synchronised parity, at the same threshold. T-4h keeps the parity forward (no futures print at 10:30 in the store) and behaves like the other snapshots once synchronised, which is itself evidence that the synchronised parity forward is usable where no futures print exists.

### Fix 2 — synchronising the quotes: **worked, and it is the fix**

- The success criterion stated in advance was "60-minute windows with adjustment approach the 10-minute χ² (≈2.0) while retaining 60-minute date coverage (28–30 of 30)". Result: 1.6 at T-1d/60 on 30 dates, 1.6 at T-2d/60 on 26, 1.9 at T-4h/60 on 30; the synchronised 10-minute windows sit at 1.2 (T-1d, 21 dates). χ² < 2 on 57% of T-1d/60 chains (V1: 14%).
- The multimodality that V1 reported goes away where it should: runs where more than half the posterior draws are multimodal fall from 43% to **0%** at T-1d/60 and 43% → 4% at T-2d/60. It does not vanish at T-4h/60 (43% → 23%), where the window covers a fifth of the option's remaining life and quotes are 21% one-cent bids; that residue is where to look next, not a market fact yet.
- Stage 14 comes back: the unconstrained posterior mean lands on the forward on 70% of T-1d/60 dates (V1 36%), |z| median 1.8 (V1 3.9; synthetic 0.74), mean − F0 1 ± 4¢. The sampler is healthier on the synchronised chains as well (bracket R̂ median 1.016 vs 1.038, ESS 246 vs 135, bracket R̂ < 1.05 on 83% vs 64% of runs) — the posterior was badly conditioned partly *because* the data were inconsistent.
- Size of the adjustment: rms 11.6¢ per quote at T-1d/60 (max 34.7¢ median), of which theta is 0.7¢ and delta 11.6¢; the estimated path ranges $0.96 within the hour (p90 $2.32). The options-implied level at the anchor agrees with the settlement to a median |offset| of 3.3¢ — an independent confirmation, using every quote, that the futures forward is the right number.
- Method choices, for the record: sticky-strike (each quote's own implied vol held fixed; carries delta, gamma and theta) was chosen over adjusting moneyness or strikes because it needs no smile model at the adjustment step and keeps the strike grid; the sticky-moneyness alternative is the `sync_m` arm (below). The path is estimated from the option trade stream because **there is no intraday futures price in the data store** (`futures_stats` carries the settlement and session extremes only; one record between 13:30 and 14:35 ET on 2026-03-12) — the handoff's premise that timestamped CL prices exist through the session does not hold for what was bought. The level is anchored at 14:29, the middle of the settlement window, and the forward used is the path's value at 14:30; the median |drift| across that minute is 1.5¢ (p90 17.3¢). At 160% vol one minute is ~20¢ of underlying, so 1-minute knots are needed (5-minute knots left χ² at 3.7 on the probe date).

- Sensitivity (`sync_m`, sticky-moneyness reprice, T-1d/60, 30 dates): χ²/strike median 1.6 vs 1.6 sticky-strike, χ² < 2 on 60% vs 57%, Gate 4 fires on 17% vs 17%, Stage 14 passes on 63% vs 70%. The two assumptions differ at second order (smile slope × move) and the tables agree to within their own noise: the conclusion does not depend on which one is used.

### Fix 3 — split-half + LOO in place of the Act II gate: **worked, with one stated limit**

- Calibration (synthetic, real grids, same sampler): on well-specified chains the split-half statistic never exceeds 3 (p95 2.65, the cutoff now in use); a planted stale quote is caught by LOO on 88% of chains and localised to the injected strike on 88%, by split-half on 62%; a bimodal truth is caught by neither (split-half 0%, LOO 12%) and by Act II on 100%. That is the division of labour the design intended: split-half and LOO see inconsistency *in the data*, Act II sees a prior the data cannot support. LOO's |z| > 3 flags a strike on 12% of clean synthetic chains, so a single LOO flag is a pointer, not a verdict.
- On the raw V1 chains the new gate fires on 79% of T-1d/60 dates (split-half max|z| median 4.9) — it agrees with the V1 gate (68%) that those chains are untrustworthy, and says why: their own halves disagree. On the synchronised chains it fires on **17%** (median 1.8), the V1 Act II gate on the same chains fires on 7%, and the fired chains have χ²/strike 3.4 against 1.5 for the quiet ones. Every synchronised 10-minute chain passes. The gate is now measuring the thing it was meant to measure and is quiet once the data are consistent.
- LOO names strikes on 70% of synchronised T-1d/60 chains (2.2 flags per chain on average, against 8.6 on raw V1 chains) — the residual inconsistency after synchronisation is concentrated in a few strikes per chain, which is the right shape for a quote-level exclusion rule rather than a date-level skip.
- Act II stays in the report. Its disagreement with Act III on synchronised chains is 0.90¢ median (V1 raw: 3.71¢), i.e. the two routes agree once the data are consistent, which is what two independent implementations are for.

### What is still not right

- **T-4h/60 keeps some multimodality (23%) and a fatter χ² tail (p90 5.3)** after synchronisation. The window is a fifth of the option's remaining life, one-cent bids are 21% of the OTM chain, and the forward there is parity (no futures print). Whether that is market structure or the last of the data problem is the next descriptive question; it is not evidence either way yet.
- **The 60-minute χ² is 1.6–1.9, not 1.1.** Half the synchronised chains still have max |resid| > 3 at T-1d/60, and LOO flags strikes on 70% of them. The adjustment is model-based (a sticky-vol reprice along an estimated path); what remains is the sum of path error (the path's own residual rms is 5.1¢), the sticky assumption, and genuinely inconsistent quotes. Quote-level exclusion (drop LOO-flagged strikes and refit) is the obvious next step and was deliberately not done in this pass — it would be tuning against chains with a known data-quality problem.
- **T-0 is still unusable** (no extractions; 75–100% one-cent bids) and the synchronised parity there has a 52¢ RMSE vs the settlement because the path estimate at expiry is erratic (theta is most of the adjustment, 14.9¢ rms). Nothing about the trade needs T-0.
- **T-2d is worth keeping now**: 26 of 30 dates extract with the futures forward (14 with parity), χ² 1.6, and its two-pair parity check is weak (RMSE 23.3¢ raw) but no longer load-bearing.
- **The prior was not touched.** Every number above is with V1's roughness prior and hyperprior, as the brief required. The bimodal blind spot of `FINDINGS_SYNTHETIC.md` §7 is untouched and the gate that would catch it (Act II) is a report line, not a gate; that remains the next question.
- **The backtest is harder than live on the parity axis**: TBBO records a strike only when it trades, so parity had 2–6 pairs here where a live book shows every listed strike two-sided. The path estimator has the same limitation (it uses trade events) and would have far more to work with live — or would be unnecessary, since a live CL quote exists at decision time.

### Foundational, restated

Still descriptive: no Kalshi price was read, no gap was computed, no trade was evaluated, and no such code exists. Every table is before/after on the same dates with the V1 pipeline re-run as the `base` arm (it reproduces V1 to the last decimal). Nothing was tuned: the prior, the sampler settings, the Gate 0 thresholds and the Act II cutoff are V1's; the only new threshold (split-half cutoff 2.65) was set on synthetic chains before the real ones were scored.
